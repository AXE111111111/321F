## 2. Problem setup [1:00]
We work on the unit square. Three edges carry Dirichlet data, and they own all four corners. The right edge carries a flux law, du/dn + beta u = r: beta = 0 is the Neumann variant, beta = 2 the Robin variant. For verification we use the manufactured solution e^(x+y) from the Problem Pack; q = e^(1+y) is positive, which confirms the outward-normal sign. Our question: how does the closure on the flux edge decide the global order, and what does it do to the linear system? This matters because, once a flux is prescribed, the closure is part of the method and can set the global accuracy on its own.

## 3. Well-posedness [0:50]
First, the continuous problem is well posed. In the weak form the Robin term adds beta times the boundary integral of u v. Boundedness follows from Cauchy–Schwarz and the trace inequality. Coercivity uses the Friedrichs inequality, which holds because the Dirichlet part has positive length — here with constant 1 over root 2 — and the Robin term is nonnegative only if beta ≥ 0. For negative beta coercivity can fail. Lax–Milgram then gives existence, uniqueness and stability. Our orders later assume a C^4 solution; near the points where the boundary type changes regularity can drop, so we claim nothing for nonsmooth data.

## 4. Discretisation [1:00]
The interior uses the standard five-point stencil. The unknowns include the right-edge nodes; Dirichlet values move to the right-hand side, so corners are never overwritten. On the right edge we compare two closures. Closure one replaces the PDE row by a backward difference of the boundary condition — a derivative row scaled by 1/h. Closure two introduces a ghost value, imposes a centred boundary condition and eliminates the ghost, so the row is still the PDE, scaled by 1/h^2, with the condition built in. Because the scalings differ, we compare residuals only in a row-scaled form.

## 5. Truncation error [1:00]
Substituting the exact solution: interior rows are O(h^2), as usual. But both boundary rows are only O(h): h/2 u_xx for the first-order row and h/3 u_xxx for the ghost row. For our solution the predicted constants are e^2/2 ≈ 3.69 and e^2/3 ≈ 2.46; substituting into the assembled matrices at N = 256 gives 3.6753 and 2.4486, so the rows are implemented as derived. The point: local order alone cannot explain why the global orders will differ.

## 6. Matrix properties [1:00]
Both matrices are nonsymmetric — eta(A) is between 0.02 and 0.08 — so one cannot run CG on A just because the Laplacian is self-adjoint. But scaling the boundary rows by 1/h for the first-order closure, or by one half for the ghost closure, makes DA exactly symmetric, and the energy identity shows it is positive definite for beta ≥ 0. For the ghost closure the one-half weights are the trapezoidal half-cell weights, so DA is the finite-volume form of the weak problem. Second, for beta ≥ 0 both A are nonsingular M-matrices: positive diagonal, nonpositive off-diagonals, irreducible diagonal dominance. That gives a discrete maximum principle.

## 7. Error bound [1:20]
The maximum principle lets us bound the error with comparison functions: phi = x(2−x)/2 ≤ 1/2 and psi = x ≤ 1. The table shows what A does to them row by row — baseline values are exact for full stencils; the reduced matrix gains nonnegative Cv near y=0,1. On ghost rows, A psi is at least 2/h, so an O(h) boundary defect only needs a multiple h/2 of psi, and the bound becomes O(h^2). On first-order rows, A psi is only about 1, so the bound stays O(h). Intuitively, a ghost row is a PDE row scaled by h^-2, so its O(h) defect is worth O(h^2) in the solution, while a first-order row imposes the flux with an O(h) error that shifts the whole solution. For our manufactured solution the leading bounds are about 3.69 h and 1.85 h^2; the measured truncation constants at N = 256 (3.6753 and 2.4486) sit just below the predicted e^2/2 and e^2/3, so the comparison table and the truncation table agree. This derivation was self-checked on 3 Nov 2026 against LeVeque Chapters 2–3 and the assembled-row numbers; we still recommend a teammate hand-check before the viva.

## 8. Code validation [1:00]
Validation uses the manufactured solution on five meshes, N = 16 to 256, with everything else fixed. We report the nodal max norm and a discrete L2 norm with trapezoidal weights on the boundary. Assembly is sparse and vectorised; submitted runs use spsolve, with CG on DA as a cross-check only. Checks: the substitution test you just saw; a scaled residual at most 8e-13 on every mesh; CG at N = 64 in 309 to 324 iterations agreeing with the direct solve to about 1e-11; and κ₂(DA) ≈ 2.18×10³ at Robin/ghost N=64 (growing like N²) — confirmed against d2_extra_log.txt dated 3 Nov 2026. The spy plots show the closures differ only in the right-edge rows. Second independent check: the course analytic bank P1–P5 through our solver (bank_vs_solver_log.txt). Ghost order is 2.00; on P2 the ghost error is round-off because the stencil is exact on quadratics; first-order is about 1, except P1 and P4 near 2.04 where u_xx(1,y)=0 kills the leading boundary truncation.

## 9. Convergence [1:20]
Here is the main result — please look at the log–log plot and the last-pair table. In both norms and both variants, the first-order closure converges at order 1.00 to 1.01 and the ghost closure at 2.00 on the last pair; the asymptotic range starts at N = 32, with every pairwise order in [1.00, 1.07] or [1.99, 2.01]. Least-squares fits over all five levels give L2 orders 1.03/2.00 (Neumann) and 1.02/2.00 (Robin), with max log-deviation at most 0.016. The first-order closure loses a full order everywhere, not only on the boundary: at N = 256 its interior maximum error, 3.37e-3, is almost as large as the boundary maximum, 3.41e-3. So the closure, not the interior five-point stencil, limits the global error — that is the answer to Direction 2. The measured errors lie below our proved bounds, by factors of about 4 and 8, as expected of comparison-function estimates. Robin errors are somewhat smaller than Neumann errors, consistent with the extra diagonal weight beta, though we have not quantified that further.

## 10. Cost vs accuracy [0:45]
Matched target ‖e‖∞ ≤ 5×10⁻³. Cost = median spsolve wall time over five runs; assembly, I/O, CG and plotting not counted. Ghost hits the target at N=16 in both variants (≈3×10⁻⁴ s).
